      Using the notch

      Phone remote

      Your agents and their cards on your phone, over your own Wi-Fi. Allow, deny, say why and answer questions from the couch, with nothing going through the internet.

      The notch can serve a small app to your phone over your Wi-Fi: every agent with what it is doing, and every approval or question card, answered with the same buttons as on the Mac. There is nothing to install from the App Store, nothing to sign up for, and nothing goes through the internet.

      Pair a phone

      1. Open the notch and click the phone button beside the gear (or choose Pair Phone… in the menu-bar menu). The notch shows a QR code. If the remote is off, click Turn On first. (It is also in Settings → Phone.)
      2. Scan the QR code with your phone’s Camera and open the link. The remote opens in Safari, already paired.
      3. In Safari, tap Share → Add to Home Screen. It now opens like an app, full screen, with the Open Notch icon. Done puts the notch back.

      Your phone has to be on the same Wi-Fi as the Mac. The address uses your Mac’s local name (your-mac.local), so it keeps working when the Wi-Fi hands the Mac a new address. Every Mac makes its own key and its own link: a phone paired with one Mac can’t reach another.

      If the Home Screen app ever asks to pair again, press Copy Link in Settings and paste it into the field the app shows (with the same Apple ID, the Mac’s clipboard reaches the phone).

      What you can do

      • See every agent: its state, what it is doing, its branch, model and context, with sub-agents under their parent.
      • Approve: Allow, Deny, Say why… (your reason goes back to the agent), and Always allow when the notch would offer it. Edits show their change in red and green.
      • Answer questions, including multi-select ones and several in a row, or type your own answer.
      • Each card shows how long until it goes back to the agent’s own prompt.

      It refreshes every two seconds while it is open.

      The same rules as the notch

      • A command with hidden characters, or one too long to read, can’t be allowed from the phone: the card says so and offers Deny only.
      • A card can’t be allowed in the first instant it exists.
      • Always allow only remembers what the card itself offers, never a shell, sudo or a compound command.

      Private by design

      • The page itself holds nothing. Everything it shows, and everything you answer, is sealed with ChaCha20-Poly1305 and a 256-bit key that only paired phones have.
      • The key reaches the phone inside the QR code’s address fragment, the part after #, which a browser never sends over the network.
      • Each request carries its time and a one-off id, so a request recorded off the Wi-Fi can’t be replayed. A device that guesses wrong twenty times in a minute is ignored for a minute.
      • Forget in Settings → Phone makes a new key: every phone paired before stops working at once.
      • Turn the remote off and nothing listens at all.

      Limits

      • Same Wi-Fi only. Away from home it can’t reach your Mac.
      • No notifications from it. iOS only lets web apps on secure (https) sites notify you, and iOS pauses an app shortly after you leave it. For a nudge when something needs you, set up ntfy (free, two minutes): with the remote on, tapping a notice opens it.
      • If your Mac’s firewall blocks incoming connections, allow Open Notch when macOS asks.
