      Approvals

      Approve before the terminal asks.

      When Claude Code or Codex wants to run something, the card is in front of you: the exact command, an edit as a red and green diff, and the buttons. Try them.

      • Always allow only what’s safe to remember. Never a shell, sudo or a piped install.
      • Never blocks you. No app, no answer in 45 s, or you’re in that terminal: its own prompt appears.
      • Can’t be timed under your pointer. A new card ignores Allow for half a second.

      Performance

      Lighter than the menu bar it lives beside.

      Native AppKit and Core Animation, no web view. Hooks and logs are watched, not polled, nothing runs while the screen is off, and the springs run in the render server.

      All questions
      What is Open Notch?

      A small macOS app that lives in the MacBook notch and shows every coding agent running on your Mac: Claude Code, Codex, Command Code, Gemini CLI, OpenCode and more. It says which one is working, which one is done, and which one needs you, and lets you approve, deny or answer without switching windows.

      Is it free?

      Yes. There is no paid tier, no account and no trial.

      Does it work on a Mac without a notch?

      Yes. On a Mac without a notch, or on an external display, it draws a virtual notch hanging from the top centre of the screen. You choose the screen in Settings → Appearance.

      What does it need?

      macOS 13 Ventura or later on Apple silicon. No other software, and no Accessibility or Screen Recording permission.

      Why does macOS say it “can’t verify” the app?

      Open Notch is not notarised by Apple yet. Right-click it in Applications, choose Open, then Open again. You only do this once.
